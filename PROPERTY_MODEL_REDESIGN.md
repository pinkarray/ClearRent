# Property Model Redesign — structure / unit / use

Status: **structure + unit BUILT 2026-08-12. `use` (commercial) still design only.**
Written 2026-07-31.
Trigger: storey buildings and commercial listings don't fit the current model.

---

## What was built, 2026-08-12

Two cases forced it: *"my property is a 5-bedroom duplex and I want to let ONE
room"*, and *"a compound with a two-bedroom on one side and a one-bedroom on the
other"* — different rents, so the compound can't be described once and cloned.

**The decision the original doc was missing.** `maxTenants` and `buildings` both
claimed to express "a room in a duplex". **`buildings` + `buildingId` is
canonical; `maxTenants` is pinned at 1.** Everything priced on a property doc is
singular — one rent, one caution deposit, one agent fee, one agreement, one
Paystack split — so two lettable units are two listings sharing a building, which
is also how they share one verified C of O. `maxTenants` stays in the model as
inert plumbing (the slot guard and occupancy CFs read it) but is now enforced at
1 in `firestore.rules`. It was NOT inert in practice: the web listing form
exposed it as a free number input, and `propertyHasOpenSlot` would have honoured
it — a second tenant accepted against the first one's terms.

**The insight that kept it small.** `propertyType` was never wrong for these two
cases; it was *under-determined*, because there was nowhere to put the structure.
Move structure onto the building and `propertyType` becomes the unit axis by
construction: `propertyType: 'room'` + `building.structure: 'duplex'` says case A
exactly. No listing-side enum change, and no migration of the live docs.

- `BuildingModel.structure` — `duplex | bungalow | storeyBuilding | blockOfFlats |
  compound | faceMeIFaceYou | detachedHouse | other`, plus optional `totalFloors`.
- `PropertyModel.unitLabel` — "Room 2", "Left flat", "BQ". Required for a grouped
  unit: without it two units of the same shape in one compound render as the
  same card twice, to the tenant AND the admin reviewer.
- `PropertyModel.floor` — `ground | 1 | 2 | …`, optional.
- **`unitScope` and `use` were deliberately NOT built.** `unitScope` would
  re-duplicate the axis `propertyType` now owns cleanly; `use` is the commercial
  branch below, and blocks neither case.

**Flow.** The grouping question was the last thing on the *pricing* step, in
ownership language ("Standalone / In a building — shares one document"). A
landlord letting one room of their own duplex saw themselves in neither option.
It is now **step 1 of 6, "What you're letting"** — the whole property, or one
unit inside it — with the building picker, structure, unit name and floor
underneath, and the ownership-document consequence following on the pricing step.

**A third axis, found by walking the journey: what is SHARED.** Once a room could
be listed, the details step still asked it for bedrooms, bathrooms, toilets,
living rooms, guest rooms and kitchens — so a let room rendered as
`1 bed · 1 bath · 1 toilet`, byte-identical to a self-contained one-bedroom flat.
Two different products at the same displayed spec, and one tenant filter matching
both. The Lagos room ladder is generated by what's shared, not by room count:

| | Gets | Shares |
|---|---|---|
| Single room | a room | bath, toilet, kitchen |
| Room & parlour | room + parlour | bath, toilet, kitchen |
| Self contain | room + own bath + kitchenette | nothing |
| Mini / 1-bed flat | bedroom + parlour + own everything | nothing |

So `PropertyModel` gained `bathroomAccess` / `toiletAccess` / `kitchenAccess`
(`private` | `shared`, plus `none` for a kitchen), and `isSingleSpace(type)` —
true for `room`, `roomAndParlour`, `selfContain`. For those three the six
counters are replaced by the access questions, and every display surface (app
card + detail, web card + detail, admin review) shows
"Room · shared bathroom · shared kitchen" instead of a bedroom count.
`roomAndParlour` and `miniFlat` joined the type list; the old tenant filter had
carried a dead `room_and_parlour` entry nothing ever wrote, which was the hint
that the rung existed with nowhere to put it.

**The type list is now filtered by the letting scope.** "The whole property"
never offers Room (a lone room IS a unit in something, and that path records what
it sits in); "one unit inside it" offers the full ladder. `duplex` stays in both
— a compound can genuinely contain a duplex, and one in production does.

**One vocabulary.** `propertyType` had four incompatible lists: the app wrote
`selfContain`/`room`, the tenant filter offered `self_contain`, `room_and_parlour`,
`studio` and `mansion` (nothing has ever written those, so those filters matched
zero properties), web wrote `self_contain`/`single_room`, and the card had no
`bungalow` case so it rendered lowercase. All surfaces now read
`PropertyModel.typeLabels` / `selectableTypes`.

**Address leak avoided.** The building NAME is address-gated: the add-property
hint asks for "Olu Compound, 12 Allen Ave", so a name routinely carries the street
the `private/location` subdoc exists to withhold until the inspection is paid.
The app shows the name only to the owner or an address-unlocked viewer; the
public web pages get the *structure* only, never the name.

**Migration.** Zero for properties (all 7 live docs already `maxTenants: 1`, and
`unitLabel`/`floor`/the access fields are absent-is-fine — a missing access
renders "Not stated" rather than guessing, since "private" would overclaim and
"shared" would undersell). The 2 live buildings need `structure`:
`functions/scripts/backfill_building_structure.js` (dry-run by default, `--apply`
to write; both are named "…Compound").

## The problem

`propertyType` is a single string doing three unrelated jobs:

| Axis | Question it answers | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Structure** | What is the building, physically? | bungalow, storey building, block of flats |
| **Unit** | What is actually being let? | the whole building, one floor, a flat, a room |
| **Use** | What will it be used for? | home, office, shop, school, hospital |

The current list — `flat`, `duplex`, `selfContain`, `bungalow`, `room`, `shop`, `office` —
mixes all three axes into one choice, so several real situations cannot be expressed:

- **A two-storey building let whole, as a school or hospital.**
  Whole-building unit + institutional use. No type says this.
- **A flat on the 2nd floor, with the other half of the same building let as an office.**
  One building, two units, *two different uses*. A single type per listing cannot say it.
- **Which floor a unit is on.** There is no `floor` field anywhere in the codebase.
  In Lagos this is not cosmetic: no lift, water pressure at the top, security on the
  ground floor, an elderly tenant.

`shop` and `office` were the clearest symptom. They are **uses**, not types — putting them
on the same axis as `bungalow` is why a shop listing ends up with a bedroom counter.

## Current state (as of 2026-07-31)

- Type chips: `add_property_screen.dart` (details step) and `edit_property_screen.dart`.
- **`shop` / `office` chips are hidden** as of 2026-07-31. The type *values* are still
  handled in `_updateAutoTitle`, `_updateAutoDescription` and `property_card.dart`, so
  restoring the two chips is all that's needed to bring them back.
- Only those two generators ever branched on `shop`/`office`. Everything else assumed a
  home: the six counters in the details step are unconditional (Bedrooms, Bathrooms,
  Toilets, Living Room, Guest Room, Kitchen), `_amenitiesList` is residential (Wardrobe,
  Kitchen Cabinets, Water Heater, Swimming Pool), plus `maxTenants` and a yearly rent
  period.
- `BuildingModel` **already** groups N unit listings under one building with a single
  ownership document verified once for all of them. The structure needed for storey
  buildings and mixed use is already there — it is only under-described to landlords
  ("In a building / Shares one document" never says *"a flat in a storey building is
  you"*).

## Proposed model

**On the building** (`BuildingModel`):
- `structure` — `bungalow` | `storeyBuilding` | `blockOfFlats` | `compound` | `other`
- `totalFloors` — int, optional

**On the listing** (`PropertyModel`):
- `unitScope` — `wholeBuilding` | `floor` | `flat` | `room`
- `floor` — `ground` | `1` | `2` | `3`… (only meaningful when in a building)
- `use` — `residential` | `office` | `shop` | `school` | `hospital` | `otherCommercial`

`propertyType` stays for now as the residential *shape* (flat / duplex / selfContain /
bungalow / room) — meaningful only when `use == residential`.

The details step then branches on **`use`**, not on a flat type list.

### Field sets by use

**Residential** — unchanged: bedrooms, bathrooms, toilets, living rooms, guest rooms,
kitchens, residential amenities.

**Commercial / institutional** — replaces the counters:
- **floor area (sq m)** — the single most important number, not captured anywhere today
- toilets
- open-plan vs partitioned
- commercial amenities: 3-phase power, parking / loading access, security, water
- service charge as its own field
- lease terms: commercial lets in Nigeria are often multi-year upfront, not the yearly
  assumption baked into the current pricing step

## Sale is a separate product — deliberately out of scope

The whole spine is rental: inspection fee → rent → tenancy → renewal → move-out → caution
deposit, with Paystack split settlement built around rent.

A sale shares only the *listing* and the *inspection*. It has no tenancy, no move-out, no
rent, no caution deposit; it does have offers and negotiation, title search, a lawyer or
escrow, Land Registry transfer, and sums perhaps 50× a year's rent — which changes the
fraud exposure completely. It is not a `forSale` flag; it is a second product.

**Minimum viable version, if wanted early:** listing intent `forSale` → **enquiry only, no
money through the platform.** Captures the listings and the demand, buyer contacts the
landlord, nothing touches the payment flow. Roughly 10% of the work and none of the risk.
The full transaction is a post-launch decision, ideally with a lawyer involved.
